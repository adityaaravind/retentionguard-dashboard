RetentionGuard
Data that should be gone, proven gone.

RetentionGuard is a generic, defensive dashboard for third-party data handling. It shows how customer order data should age out after a retention window, how deletion can be proven instead of merely promised, and how bulk personal-data exports should be blocked before they leave a system.

Synthetic data onlyNo backend requiredNo installRuns entirely in your browser

If you opened this page without reading anything else, start here: this is a demonstration, not a real company's system. No real customer data is used, nothing is sent anywhere, and no account is changed by anything on this page.

What is this?

When a company uses a third party to store, ship, or analyse orders, that third party often ends up holding customer names, emails, phone numbers, addresses, and order numbers long after the order is finished. If that data is kept indefinitely, a later breach exposes far more people than it should. If a vendor says “we deleted it” but cannot prove it, an auditor, a merchant, and a customer all have to take that on trust.

1. Age it out

Orders pass a 90-day retention window. After that, personal fields are anonymized, unless the order is in a visible returns/refunds exception queue.

2. Prove deletion

Each sweep issues a deletion certificate linked by a hash chain. You can verify it, or simulate tampering and watch verification fail.

3. Guard exports

Bulk personal-data exports, and non-parameterized queries, are blocked and alerted. Limited, parameterized exports are allowed.

4. Check vendors

A vendor's deletion claim is checked against counts and sample records. A mismatch is flagged, not filed away.

5. Scope a breach fast

Given a compromised system and date range, generate the affected synthetic customers, exposed fields, and a notification draft, instead of discovering the full scope weeks later.

6. Keep it auditable

Every step above produces a record you can show: counts, certificates, blocked exports, and vendor checks, in one place.

How to use it — in 2 minutes

  1. Look at the Overview below: how many synthetic orders are active, due for deletion, anonymized, or held as exceptions.
  2. Click “Run retention sweep” in the Order Vault. Watch due orders become anonymized and a deletion certificate appear.
  3. Click “Verify certificate”, then “Simulate tampering” to see why a hash chain matters.
  4. Try the BI Export Guard with the default 5,000-row, full personal-data query. It should be blocked. Then lower the rows, remove personal data, and tick “Parameterized query” to see an allowed export.
  5. Check Vendor attestation, then Generate a breach-scope list and download the synthetic CSV.

No sign-in, no setup, and no data leaves your browser. Refreshing the page resets the demo to its starting state.

About this project

Scope

RetentionGuard is a front-end demonstration of data-lifecycle controls for third-party order handling: retention windows, deletion proof, export guarding, vendor attestation checks, and breach scoping. It is intentionally generic and does not name, target, or connect to any real company or vendor.

Methodology

All 120 orders are generated in the browser from a small synthetic name list. Ages and statuses are assigned by rule, not taken from real records. The retention sweep, certificate hash, export guard, and breach-scope list all operate only on that synthetic sample.

Limitations

This is a demonstration, not a production compliance system. The certificate hash is a simple illustrative hash, not a production cryptographic proof. The export guard, vendor checks, and breach-scope tool do not connect to real databases, identity providers, or vendor systems. Do not use this page with real customer data, and do not present synthetic numbers as real incident numbers.

Who this is for

IT support and operations teams who own vendor and data-handling workflows, security and compliance reviewers who need to see deletion evidence, and anyone evaluating how retention, export controls, and breach scoping should work together in one place.
